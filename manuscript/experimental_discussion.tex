\section{Interpretation of the experiments}
\label{sec:experimental-interpretation}

The main experiment finds one simple pattern.  One quadratic controls the
interior of the cross-wedge.  Three affine boundary lines have extra quotient
directions.  The same failures remain when we raise the Fourier cutoff.  They
are separate filtration cases, not truncation errors.

The proofs explain the following parts of this pattern for all admissible
parameters.  The first-Hasse-jet formula explains why the $D=2$ block fails
in the new wedge.  Exact filtration shows that the three boundary directions
remain nonzero in the quotient.  The perfect Hasse-quotient pairing and theta
skew-adjointness connect the two obstructions.  These theorems do not give
every boundary coefficient.  The missing steps are the factorial block
reduction in Conjecture~\ref{conj:factorial-block-reduction} and two
triangular containments for the common endpoint core.  We now compute the
pre-low-point and reset jet terms for all admissible parameters.  Both lie
below the relevant cutoffs.

The evidence is strongest on the third boundary line.  We first observed the
one-block shape in $78$ cells.  A separate blind audit tested $652$ cells.  A
second audit used $186$ ordinary forms in a two-dimensional cusp-form space.
Together, the two later audits made $2{,}512$ tests.  Every test returned the
same form-independent factorial scalar.  This is strong finite evidence for
the stated conjecture.  It does not replace the missing symbolic proof.

The study has three limits.  First, the main cross-wedge table ends at
$p=43$.  The separate third-line test reaches $p=79$, and the symbolic index
audit reaches $p=101$.  Second, the broad record uses level-one ordinary
forms.  It does not test supersingular or higher-level behavior.  Third, the
two triangular coefficient identities have exact finite support, proved
endpoint independence, and proved low-point jet reductions.  The uniform
containment of the remaining core series is still open.  These limits define
the next tests and proof problems.
