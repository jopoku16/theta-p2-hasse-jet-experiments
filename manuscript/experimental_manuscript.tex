\begin{abstract}
Some weights in theta cycles modulo $p^2$ are unknown.  We study one such
region.  We prove that its weight-lowering obstruction is a first Hasse jet.
We also prove a perfect pairing for the Hasse quotients and show that theta is
skew-adjoint for this pairing.  Our minimal-weight projection and filtration
arguments separate the boundary directions.  Exact computations classify
$692$ ordinary cross-wedge cells.  One shifted quadratic matches all $458$
interior obstructions.  The other $234$ cells lie on three boundary lines.
Two independent tests give $2{,}512$ matches with one factorial boundary
scalar.  On the two triangular lines, we prove the unit and finite-block
terms and compute the low-point jets.  The full shifted-conic law still needs
one factorial block identity and two triangular containments.  These three
identities remain conjectural.  We provide exact code, negative controls, and
tests that can find a failure.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} theta cycles; modular forms modulo prime powers;
experimental mathematics; Hasse invariant; Bockstein class; weight
filtration.

\noindent\textbf{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:}
11F33, 11F11, 11Y40.

\section{Introduction}

The theta cycle of a modular form modulo a prime is classical.  A new
first-order obstruction appears modulo $p^2$.  A form may lower in weight
modulo $p$ but fail to lower in the same way modulo $p^2$.
Ahlgren, Raum, and Richter determined the initial segment and gave later
exact values and bounds \cite{ARR2026}.  Some low points in the next band
remain unknown for general primes.

The basic filtration theory modulo $p$ comes from work of
Swinnerton-Dyer and Serre \cite{SwDyer1973,Serre1973}.  Katz gave a
geometric theory of modular forms in characteristic $p$, including the
Hasse invariant and the theta operator \cite{Katz1973}.  Later work on
modular weights and companion forms developed this viewpoint further
\cite{Edixhoven1992,ColemanVoloch1992}.

We ask one question.  Does the quadratic pattern in the resolved wedge remain
valid after reflection into the next wedge?  Our first candidate failed on
every tested $D=0$ cell.  We then added the missing minimal-weight projection.
In the interior, the lower Bockstein class followed
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-shifted-conic-experimental}
 i'^2+(k-1)i'-(n+1)(n+2)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.
\end{equation}
Every disagreement occurred on one of three affine lines.  The full residuals
suggested one factorial block and two triangular endpoint formulas.

We explain how we found and tested this formula.  Every calculation uses
exact modular arithmetic and Sturm-safe remainders.  The tests include a
failed baseline, a blind extension to new primes, and a separate calculation
in a two-dimensional cusp-form space.  We then prove the geometry behind the
experiment.  The weight-lowering obstruction is a first Hasse jet.  The
ambient quotient has a perfect pairing, and theta is skew-adjoint for this
pairing.  Multiplication by the integral Hasse inclusion commutes with theta
modulo $p^2$.  We also construct the minimal-weight projection.  Exact
filtration separates the proposed boundary directions.

Three identities remain open.  The direct formula should reduce to a
factorial block identity.  That identity would give the generic
shifted-conic law and the observed scalar on the third line.  Two triangular
containments remain open on the other boundary lines.  We compute their
low-point jet terms for all admissible parameters.  We state the three
remaining claims as conjectures because our computations cover only a finite
range.

The open identities are explicit coefficient problems in known block bases.
Their parameters satisfy short exact recurrences.  Each identity also has a
direct finite-field test.  These facts suggest possible proof methods and
make counterexamples easy to find.  They do not replace a proof for all
admissible primes.

\paragraph{Scope.}
We work with ordinary level-one forms and primes $p\geq5$.  The main census
ends at $p=43$; separate audits reach $p=79$ and $p=101$.  We do not treat
non-ordinary forms, higher level, or the three open block identities as
settled cases.

\subsection{The main idea in plain language}

A modular form has a Fourier expansion, which is an infinite list of
coefficients.  The theta operator changes this list by multiplying the
coefficient of $q^n$ by $n$.  A theta cycle records the smallest possible
weight after each repeated use of theta.  The pattern modulo $p$ is well
understood.  Modulo $p^2$, two forms can agree modulo $p$ but differ at the
next $p$-adic level.  That first difference is the obstruction studied here.

Most tested positions follow one shifted quadratic rule.  Three straight
boundary lines need extra correction terms.  The geometric results explain
where the obstruction lives and why the boundary terms are independent.  The
exact computations then identify the formulas that a complete proof must
establish.  Three coefficient identities are still open, so the paper keeps
the final shifted-conic law as a conjectural application.

The experiments identify the classification problem and the three boundary
cases.  The theorems explain why the candidate has this form.  They also
reduce the open part to explicit identities.

\subsection{Main results and limits}

The results fall into three groups.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Uniform theorems.}  We prove the first-Hasse-jet description,
      perfect Hasse-quotient duality, theta skew-adjointness, the explicit
      minimal-weight projection, separation of the boundary directions,
      exact affine recurrences for the factorial target, and exclusion of
      the finite block from both triangular endpoints.  We also give a
      closed formula for the reset jet and prove that both low-point jet
      corrections lie below the endpoint cutoffs.
\item \textbf{Exact finite results.}  The main census has $692$ cells.  Two
      later tests give $2{,}512$ exact matches with the proposed third-line
      scalar.  A separate index audit checks $210{,}176$ parameter cells
      through $p=101$.
\item \textbf{Open claims.}  One factorial block identity and two triangular
      block-basis containments are still conjectural.  The full
      shifted-conic law is conditional on these identities.
\end{enumerate}
We use this split throughout the paper.  A finite calculation is never stated
as a theorem for all primes.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

Ahlgren--Raum--Richter \cite{ARR2026} prove the initial segment, further
exact values or bounds modulo $p^2$, and a quadratic condition for the
exceptional positions in their resolved region.  Our code also checks the
resolved $D=2$ subwedge, whose conic is
\[
 i'^2+(k-1)i'-n(n+1)=0.
\]
We study the complementary $D=0$ subwedge.  Reflection changes the constant
term to $(n+1)(n+2)$.  A new first-Hasse-jet projection is needed before this
expression gives the actual obstruction.  This is not a restatement of
Ahlgren--Raum--Richter.  The geometric pairing alone also does not prove the
shifted-conic law.

\section{Remaining symbolic identities}

Theorem~\ref{thm:explicit-periodic-first-jet} constructs the minimal
mod-$p$ representative in characteristic zero and computes its normal
coefficient along the Hasse divisor.  A complete cross-wedge theorem now
requires the factorial block reduction in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:factorial-block-reduction} and the two triangular
associated-graded containments in
Corollary~\ref{cor:remaining-endpoint-target}.  The residual directions,
their independence, the finite-block endpoint contribution, and both
low-point jet corrections are already proved.
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-common-core} links the two remaining endpoint
formulas by one exact theta recurrence.  The open task is to place the core
series $T_s$ and its theta image in the stated triangular spans, and to
simplify the factorial operator formula in the direct block basis.

On the third line, that simplification predicts
\[
 \mathcal B_i-\mathcal L_i
 =-m(k-2m-2)!
 X^{k-2m-1}\theta^{p-k}f.
\]
The displayed block is nonzero by
Proposition~\ref{prop:l3-block-nonzero}, and the scalar is a unit by
Proposition~\ref{prop:l3-closed-scalar-unit}.  Thus the unresolved point is
not nonvanishing, but identification of the computed scalar with the formal
block reduction for every admissible parameter set.

\section{Conclusion}

The exact experiments find a shifted quadratic inside the next theta-cycle
wedge and three extra residuals on its boundary.  The geometry and filtration
results put these residuals in the predicted quotient directions.  The first
Hasse jet and the direct block calculation reduce the open problem to three
coefficient identities.  The finite block has no triangular endpoint.  One
exact theta recurrence links the two remaining endpoint cores.  Closed
formulas remove the pre-low-point and reset jets from the open part.  The
factorial block reduction and the two triangular containments remain open.
